# Protein.tn responsive auth reference prompt

Create a polished, high-fidelity responsive UI mockup board for the Protein.tn ecommerce authentication experience, using the supplied current login page as visual context but redesigning its layout and spacing. Show two finished artboards side by side: desktop at 1440 × 900 and mobile at 390 × 844.

Use the existing Protein.tn design system: clean white surfaces, warm peach background, vivid Protein.tn orange accents, near-black typography, soft gray borders, subtle shadows, rounded corners, and the existing realistic Tunisian athlete wearing the Protein.tn shirt. Keep the photography natural and credible. Do not redesign the brand logo or form contents.

Desktop: center a compact card approximately 1160 × 650 with an even 50/50 split. Put the white login form on the left and the peach athlete image on the right. Move the form content upward enough to feel balanced, use a clear logo/back-link header, strong but restrained typography, generous usable form widths, 48–52 px controls, and one orange primary button. Use the lower part of the form panel for a compact three-item trust row: delivery in 24–48 hours, payment on delivery, and loyalty points. Place only a small caption in unused image space; do not use a large dark overlay and do not cover the athlete.

Mobile: use a 220–240 px peach athlete banner at the top with a circular back control. Place a compact white form card so it overlaps the banner slightly, with the logo inside the card. Keep the form and account switch immediately followed by the trust row, without large blank white areas. Maintain 44 px minimum touch targets, readable spacing, no horizontal overflow, and a clear visual hierarchy. Show the athlete’s face in the crop.

The result should feel energetic, premium, trustworthy, simple, and conversion-focused. Avoid decorative gradients, dense copy, oversized typography, excess empty space, glassmorphism, and unnecessary UI decoration. Use accurate French UI copy and make both artboards production-realistic rather than conceptual.
