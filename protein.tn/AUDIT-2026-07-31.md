## 2. Why Search Console hasn't shown it yet

GSC data ends **29 July**. The fixes landed **28–31 July**. The reporting window has not covered
them yet — this is arithmetic, not optimism.

The first signal is already visible on the last day in the export:

| date | clicks | impressions | avg position |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24–28 Jul (typical) | 32–52 | 1,247–1,311 | 9.5–11.4 |
| **29 Jul** | 40 | **2,756** | **25.5** |

Impressions **more than doubled** and average position fell to 25.5. That combination is the
signature of *many new pages entering the index at first-time positions* — which is exactly what
the /shop crawler fix did (Googlebot went from seeing 12 products to 303). New pages enter deep and
climb; they drag the average down on the way in. One day is not a trend, but it is the right shape.

## 4. Bugs found and fixed today

**a. `/proteines` lead heading reads "ROTÉINES EN TUNISIE"** — the `P` is missing. Stored broken in
`categories.description_fr`; comes back broken from
`GET /api/productsByCategoryId/proteines`, so both the crawler view and the human view render it.
This is the H2 introducing the page that targets *protéine tunisie* (2,241 impressions, position
12.5) and *protéines tunisie* (438, 15.5). Almost certainly lost in the WordPress → Angular → Next
migrations. Fixed by migration `2026_07_31_000002_repair_truncated_category_heading`.
All 88 listing pages were checked for the same corruption — this is the only one.

**b. `/shop` served two different `<h1>`s.** Googlebot got
"Boutique — Protéines & Compléments Alimentaires en Tunisie"; everyone else got "Tous nos produits".
Divergent H1s on one URL is what turns defensible dynamic rendering into cloaking, and the human
version named no product, category or country. Both now use the descriptive heading.

**c. `/shop` was uncacheable and shipped ~1 MB of HTML per request.** Two independent causes:

- `ShopPageClient` calls `useSearchParams()` at its top level with no Suspense boundary *above* it,
  so Next bailed the route out of static rendering. `export const revalidate = 300` never applied:
  the live page answers `Cache-Control: private, no-cache, no-store, max-age=0` and Cloudflare
  reports `cf-cache-status: DYNAMIC`. **No CDN cache rule can help a page that says no-store** —
  this is why the Cloudflare rules didn't improve mobile. Fixed by wrapping in `<Suspense>`.
- `GET /api/all_products` **ignores `per_page` and `page`**. `ApisController::allProducts` ends in
  `$query->get()` with no pagination, unlike `productsByCategoryId` which correctly uses
  `->paginate($perPage)`. The frontend asks for 24 and receives all 303 (679 kB of JSON), which is
  then serialized into the page: **799 kB of the 1,029 kB `/shop` document is the RSC payload
  carrying 303 products, while 12 are displayed.** NOT fixed — see §6.

## 6. Not done, and why

**`/api/all_products` pagination is not a safe unilateral fix.** `ShopPageClient` performs *all*
faceting — search, brand, price, sort — client-side over the full catalogue it was handed.
Paginating the endpoint to 24 would silently reduce filtering to whatever page 1 happens to hold.
The fix is real but it is an architecture change (server-side faceting, or a slimmer
listing projection), and it needs to be decided rather than slipped in.

Cheaper interim option: keep returning every product but strip the listing projection down to the
~8 fields a card actually renders. The payload carries `description_fr`, full `cover` objects and
more that no card uses.

## 8. Product data enrichment — the honest version

Two genuinely free, licensed sources, both suitable:

- **Open Food Facts** — no API key, no rate limit for reasonable use, ODbL. 3M+ products including
  supplements; barcode lookup returns nutrition per 100g, ingredients, allergens.
- **NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database (DSLD)** — 200k+ US supplement labels, free API, public
  domain. Closer to this catalogue than OFF, because it is supplement labels specifically.

**What this will and will not do.** Filling in Supplement Facts makes pages genuinely more useful
and unlocks richer structured data. But it does *not* create unique content — the same facts are on
every competitor's page. Ranking lift comes from what only we can write: Tunisian pricing context,
what is actually in stock here, comparisons across the brands we carry.

**Scraping big competitor sites for descriptions would actively hurt.** Duplicated manufacturer
copy is the single most common reason a product page gets classified "Crawled — currently not
indexed", which is the bucket 1,239 of our pages already sit in. Copy their text and we compete
with the original using their words.
