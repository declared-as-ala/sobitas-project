# Adding Arabic and English to protein.tn — Implementation Plan

**Date:** 2026-08-01 · **Status:** decision document, not a build ticket

---

## 1. Read this first: the risk that decides everything else

**You do not have a translation problem. You have a "there is no second language anywhere in your data" problem.**

The database has exactly one text column per field. `designation_fr` is not the French version of a name — it is *the* name; the `_fr` suffix is dead weight from the old Voyager backend. A repo-wide search across the Laravel app for any `_ar` or `_en` column returns **zero matches**. There is no translations table, no `locale` column, no translation package installed. Measured against the live API, the content that would need a second version is roughly:

| Content | Rows | Words to translate (per language) |
|---|---|---|
| Products | 303 | ~149,000 |
| Blog articles | 224 published | ~203,000 |
| Categories | 6 | 5,219 (measured exactly) |
| Sub-categories | 41 | ~27,000 |
| Pages, FAQs, brands, footer | ~90 | ~5,000 |
| **Total** | | **≈390,000 words per language** |

Two languages is **~780,000 words**. For scale: that is roughly ten full-length novels.

### What Google actually says about machine-translating your way out of that

Most SEO advice on this is out of date, so here is the current text, verified against the live pages:

- The line everyone quotes — *"Text translated by an automated tool without human review or curation before publishing"* — **was deleted from Google's spam policies in March 2024.** It is gone. Google went further in June 2024 and removed its own advice about robots.txt-blocking auto-translated pages, explicitly "to align with our spam policy update in March 2024."
- The rule that replaced it is **scaled content abuse**: *"many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users… typically focused on creating large amounts of unoriginal content that provides little to no value to users, **no matter how it's created**."* Google states the test applies *"whether automation or humans are involved."*
- Translation survives in the policy only once, and narrowly: *"Scraping feeds, search results, or other content to generate many pages (including through automated transformations like synonymizing, translating, or other obfuscation techniques), where little value is provided to users."* The predicate act there is **scraping someone else's content**. Translating your own catalogue is not that.

**So: bulk machine translation is not automatically a penalty, and paying a human to skim it is not automatically a cure.** Anyone who tells you either of those is quoting a policy Google deleted two years ago.

### The three things that *are* genuinely dangerous

1. **Untranslated main content = duplicates, by Google's own definition.** *"Localized versions of a page are only considered duplicates if the main content of the page remains untranslated."* Translated pages are not duplicates. Half-translated ones are.
2. **The half-translated build is the specific pattern Google warns about.** *"Translating only the boilerplate text of your pages while keeping the bulk of your content in a single language… can create a bad user experience if the same content appears multiple times in search results with various boilerplate languages."* This is exactly what you get if you translate the header, footer and buttons (the ~830 UI strings) but leave the 149,000 words of product copy in French. That is the cheap version of this project, and it is the version Google names.
3. **It is the wrong medicine for the diagnosed illness.** Your July 2026 diagnosis was: technical SEO is clean on all 391 pages, **content depth is the blocker**. 44 of 88 listing pages are under 300 words. A second language doubles your URL surface from ~391 to ~780 — it adds *breadth* to a site whose problem is *depth*. Even done perfectly, it treats the wrong thing.

**Conclusion carried into the rest of this plan:** every stage below is designed so that no stage ever ships a page whose main content is not genuinely in the language its URL claims.

## 2. Three facts that reframe the question

**(a) Arabic content already exists on your site and one piece of it is winning.** Of your 224 published articles, **32 have Arabic titles and Arabic bodies** — real Arabic, written by a human, already live. One of them ranks at **position 5.73 with 1,969 impressions**. You are not starting from zero on Arabic; you are starting from a working proof that is undeclared and unmaintained (all 32 have `content_lang` = NULL, and 31 have raw Arabic slugs containing spaces and `?` characters).

**(b) Your English "demand" is not demand for an English site.** 9,750 impressions, **18 clicks**. `omega 3 fish oil`: 2,827 impressions, **0 clicks**, position 7.46. You already rank on page one for these. Tunisians type English *product nouns inside French queries* — Google autocomplete for Tunisia returns `whey protein tunisie`, `whey isolate tunisie`, `straps gym tunisie`. The fix for 0% CTR at position 7 is not an English website. It is putting those English nouns in the title and description of the French page that already ranks.

**(c) The Arabic head term is a trap.** In Tunisia, `بروتين` autocompletes to `بروتين الشعر` (hair protein), `شامبو بروتين`, `بروتين الشعر البرازيلي` — keratin hair treatments, not sports nutrition. An Arabic page built on that word attracts hairdressing shoppers and tanks its own engagement. Viable Arabic terms with Tunisia intent are narrower: `واي بروتين`, `مكملات غذائية في تونس`, `كرياتين تونس`, `بروتين لزيادة الوزن في تونس`.

Also worth knowing: **every tier-1 Tunisian e-commerce site is French-only.** tunisianet, mytek, mycare, bonheur, parapharmacie — all `<html lang="fr">`, no hreflang, no Arabic. Two of them do ~1M visits/month. That is not proof Arabic can't work; it is proof nobody with real scale in your market has found it worth doing. Treat the Arabic bet as unproven and structure it as a cheap test, not a rebuild.

## 3. The URL structure decision

**Recommendation: subdirectories on the existing domain — French stays exactly where it is at the root, Arabic goes to `protein.tn/ar/…`, English later (if ever) to `/en/…`.**

Three reasons, in order of importance:

1. **The 391 French URLs never move.** This is the whole argument. Moving French to `/fr/` means redirecting every ranking page on a site that has already lived through an indexing crisis, an empty-ISR-bake incident, and a Router-Cache regression. The upside of that migration is *symmetry*. The downside is your revenue. Don't.
2. **You already own the strongest geo-signal there is.** Google: a ccTLD like `.tn` is *"a strong signal to both users and search engines that your site is explicitly intended for a certain country."* You get country targeting for free. Buying `protein.ma` or similar would add cost and nothing else.
3. **Google's own comparison table gives the subdirectory the only maintenance advantage.** Of its four options, subdirectories are the sole entry tagged *"Low maintenance (same host)"*, and the only option Google explicitly rejects is URL parameters (`?lang=ar`) — *"Not recommended."* Note Google never says "we recommend subdirectories"; that one table cell is the whole basis, and it is enough.

**Language codes:** use plain `fr` and `ar`. **Not `fr-TN`.** A region subtag only ever *narrows* who matches — `fr-TN` means "French, but only for people Google geolocates in Tunisia," which excludes the diaspora, travellers, and anyone on a mis-geolocated mobile carrier IP. It buys nothing you don't already get from `.tn`. Set `x-default` to the French URL. You currently emit a degenerate hreflang block on three page templates (`{'fr-TN': self, 'x-default': self}`) that points only at itself — that is inert markup on a single-language site; replace it when Arabic ships, and fix `og:locale` which is wrongly set to `fr_FR`.

**On canonicals — the corrected version of the usual advice.** You will be told "every language version must self-canonicalize or hreflang is silently disabled." That is stronger than anything Google publishes. What Google actually writes is: *"If you're using hreflang elements, make sure to specify a canonical page in the **same language**"* — same language, not same URL. And the documented cause of hreflang being ignored is **missing return links**, not canonicals: *"If two pages don't both point to each other, the tags will be ignored."* The practical rule still holds — **never canonicalize `/ar/x` back to the French `/x`** — but know the real mechanism: the Arabic page simply won't get indexed, and you'll see it in Search Console as "Duplicate, Google chose a different canonical," not as an hreflang error. Correct hreflang actually *helps* canonicalization: Google states it *"prefers URLs that are part of hreflang clusters."*

## 4. Staged rollout

Ordered by traffic upside ÷ risk. Every stage is shippable and valuable on its own, and every stage stops being worth doing if the one before it produced nothing.

---

### Stage 0 — Declare the Arabic you already have · **½–1 day**

**Ships:** `content_lang = 'ar'` and `content_text_direction = 'rtl'` set on the 32 Arabic articles (columns already exist, migration `2026_04_17_120000`, currently NULL/`auto` on all 32). Corrected slugs for the 31 articles whose URLs contain literal Arabic spaces and `?`, with 301s from the old ones. `og:locale` corrected `fr_FR` → `fr_TN`.

**Upside:** the ranking Arabic article (position 5.73, 1,969 impressions) starts serving `lang="ar" dir="rtl"` in the initial HTML instead of `lang="fr"`. Small, but it is the first honest signal about Arabic you will have ever sent.

**Risk:** near zero. No new URLs, no routing change, no build change.

**Proof it worked (not "deploy was green"):** `curl` the live article URL and confirm the served HTML — not the hydrated DOM — contains `lang="ar"` and `dir="rtl"`. Confirm the old slug 301s and the new one returns 200. Screenshot the article on a real Android phone and confirm the Arabic letters are *joined*, not disconnected stubs.

---

### Stage 1 — English nouns in French titles · **2–4 days** · *highest upside ÷ risk in this document*

**Ships:** rewritten `meta_title` / `meta_description` / H1 on the ~30 pages already earning English impressions, embedding the English product nouns users actually type — `whey protein`, `whey isolate`, `omega 3 fish oil`, `gainer`, `BCAA`, `Gold Standard`, `straps` — inside otherwise-French copy. Nothing is translated. No page changes language. No URL is created.

**Upside:** 9,750 impressions currently converting at **0.18%**. Getting to a normal 2–3% CTR is roughly **200–290 additional clicks per month** from pages that already rank. That is the single largest measurable gap in your Search Console data, and it needs no code.

**Ceiling, honestly:** a few hundred clicks a month, not thousands. It is a CTR fix, not a ranking fix.

**Risk:** low, but not zero — you are editing titles on ranking pages. Change 5 pages, wait 21 days, read the CTR delta in Search Console, then do the rest. Do not batch all 30.

**Effort note:** these values live in the `products` / `categs` / `sous_categories` tables and are editable in Filament today. No developer required after the first five are done as examples.

---

### Stage 2 — Ten to fifteen hand-written Arabic guides · **3–6 weeks, mostly writing** · *the actual test of the Arabic hypothesis*

**Ships:** 10–15 new Arabic articles on the existing `/blog/` route — same route, same template, no `[lang]` segment, no routing change. Each targets an Arabic query that demonstrably has Tunisia intent (`مكملات غذائية في تونس`, `واي بروتين`, `كرياتين تونس`, `بروتين لزيادة الوزن في تونس`) and links into the existing French product pages. Each carries `content_lang='ar'`, `dir='rtl'`, an ASCII slug, and a real Arabic `meta_title`/`meta_description`.

**Written by a human who speaks Tunisian Arabic.** Not translated from French. This is the one place in the plan where cost is unavoidable and non-negotiable — a native writer, roughly 1,200–1,800 words per guide.

**Upside:** you already have a proof point at position 5.73. Arabic supplement queries in your Search Console — `مكمل غذائي` 329, `مصادر البروتين` 299, `كرياتين` 282 impressions — are being answered today by a thin Arabic corpus, which is exactly why a French page can still rank for them. That thinness is the opportunity: an Arabic page that exists can take those SERPs outright.

**Why this shape and not a catalogue:** URL surface grows ~3%, not ~100%. It adds depth (the diagnosed blocker) rather than breadth. It touches zero transactional templates, so no RTL work on checkout, no price-slider bugs, no carousel rework. And it dodges the `بروتين` hair-protein trap by construction, because you choose the terms.

**Risk:** low. Worst case you have spent writer money on 15 pages that don't rank — which is itself the answer to the bigger question.

**Proof it worked:** **90 days of Search Console before any further commitment.** The decision rule, set now, in writing: *if these 15 Arabic pages do not earn meaningful Arabic impressions and clicks in 90 days, Stage 3 and Stage 4 are cancelled.* A full Arabic catalogue would not have earned them either.

Incidental fact that should sharpen this stage: you have **224 published articles and roughly 40 indexed.** Before writing 15 new ones, find out why 180 existing ones aren't in the index. It may be the cheapest content win available.

---

### Stage 3 — The `/ar/` routing spine · **4–8 weeks of engineering** · *only if Stage 2 earned its 90 days*

This is where the work stops being content and starts being surgery. Four things in your codebase actively fight a locale prefix, and all four are blockers, not chores.

**Blocker 1 — the site currently deletes `/ar` and `/en` URLs on sight.**
`src/middleware.ts:294-297` matches `/^\/(en|ar)(\/.*)?$/` and permanently 301s the prefix away, commented *"Locale is client-side now."* Independently, `redirects.js:15-16` declares `/en → /` as a permanent Next.js redirect, and next.config redirects run *before* middleware — so `/en` is 308'd before middleware even sees it. Both are permanent and therefore cached; any `/ar` URL already crawled stays poisoned in intermediary caches after the code is removed.

**Blocker 2 — the crawler-rewrite mechanism is counted by URL segments, so a prefix silently destroys it.**
`middleware.ts:361` treats *exactly two segments* as a product and *exactly one segment* as a category. Under `/ar/`: a product (`/ar/proteines/whey-gold`) becomes three segments and matches **neither** rule, so Googlebot silently stops getting the zero-JS server-rendered product view on every product page. A category (`/ar/whey`) becomes two segments and matches the **product** rule — bots get rewritten to a product that doesn't exist and receive a 404. And the Arabic homepage `/ar` matches the single-segment category rule, so it rewrites to `/x-crawler/category/ar`, which 404s + noindexes for crawlers while humans see a normal 200. That last one is precisely the `/pack-builder` failure mode already documented in `productUrl.ts:118-122`.

**Blocker 3 — nineteen locale-blind path checks, including your only no-deploy tool.**
Middleware has 9 exact-path checks and 10 anchored regexes, none of which recognise a prefix. The worst is `middleware.ts:165`: `getAdminRedirect(pathname)` does an **exact-match Map lookup** against unprefixed rows from Filament's Redirections table. On any `/ar/` URL, **zero** admin-configured redirects fire — that is the store owner's only way to clean up 404s without a deploy, and it would silently stop working for the new language.

**Blocker 4 — the canonical guard fails open and starts rejecting valid canonicals.**
`canonical.ts:214` checks the *first* segment against a 27-entry blocklist; with a prefix the first segment is always `ar`, so the guard never matches and stops catching bad admin canonicals — it fails **silently open**. And `canonical.ts:217` rejects any canonical deeper than 2 segments, which is every localized product URL.

**The design that makes this tractable:** strip the locale prefix into a variable at the very top of middleware, run **all existing rules against the unprefixed path**, then re-apply the prefix on the way out. That converts four separate rewrites into one change, and keeps the existing crawler/redirect/canonical logic as the single source of truth for both languages. `buildCanonicalUrl()` gains a locale parameter (it has none today; all 20 call sites are known).

**Also in scope for Stage 3:**
- **Static rendering must be defended, not assumed.** Adding `generateStaticParams` to the `[lang]` layout is necessary and *not sufficient*. You also need `dynamicParams = false` (default is `true` — otherwise `/de/`, `/xx/` and every bot probe render on demand as real 200s), a `generateStaticParams` that provably returns a non-empty array in CI (returning nothing makes the route dynamic **with a green build** — the same failure class as the 403 empty-bake), and zero use of `cookies()`/`headers()`/`searchParams` anywhere in the `[lang]` tree. Because `[lang]` sits above everything, **one poisoned ancestor takes all pages in all languages dynamic.** Detect language in middleware (it runs before the cache and does not affect rendering mode) — never by reading `headers()` in a layout. **Verification is the `next build` route table: every localized route must print `○ Static`, not `ƒ Dynamic`. Diff that table before and after.**
- **Sitemaps.** `sitemapData.ts` (523 lines) and `sitemapXml.ts` (174 lines) contain zero occurrences of locale/hreflang/alternate. Every entry needs `xhtml:link rel="alternate"` children, or three sitemap sets.
- **Googlebot will never see your language detection.** Google crawls from the USA and *"sends HTTP requests without setting Accept-Language."* Every Arabic URL must therefore be independently linked, in the sitemap, and reciprocally hreflang'd — auto-redirecting by browser language is explicitly warned against (*"Avoid automatically redirecting users from one language version of a site to a different language version"*). Also verify the root redirect's behaviour **through Cloudflare**, not locally: if the edge caches one 307 for `/` without varying, the first visitor's language gets pinned for everyone including Googlebot. You have been burned by a Cloudflare/origin interaction before.
- **Backend locale plumbing.** `SetRequestLocale` middleware already runs on every API request and resolves fr/en/ar — and exactly one class in the whole app consumes the result (the order-confirmation email). The frontend already sends the locale three ways on every request. Wire it through as a **`?lang=` query parameter, not the `X-Locale` header**: `CacheApiResponse.php:31` keys the cache on `md5($request->fullUrl())` and `AddCacheHeaders.php:24` sets `s-maxage=300` with **no `Vary` header** — a header-based locale would let the first Arabic request be cached and served to French visitors by both Laravel and Cloudflare. Also fix the dead cookie fallback: frontend writes `sobitas_locale`, backend reads `protein_locale`.
- **RTL.** 386 direction-unsafe Tailwind occurrences across 71 files (`left-` 93, `mr-` 69, `right-` 66, `pl-` 54, `ml-` 44, …). 103 sit inside 46 vendored shadcn primitives (fix upstream/plugin), leaving ~255 hand-written. Tailwind 3.4 already ships logical utilities (`ms-`/`me-`/`ps-`/`pe-`/`start-`/`end-`) — the codebase uses **four** of them. The 38 hand-written `html[dir="rtl"]` override rules in `globals.css:196-278` must be **deleted in the same commit** as the codemod, not extended: they cover only spacing values 1–4, miss every responsive variant, and use `!important`. Then hand-fix what logical properties cannot cover: the hero slider's index math (`scrollLeft` goes **negative** in RTL, so the dots freeze on slide 1), the carousel's arrow glyphs and positions, arrow-key handlers (in RTL, ArrowRight means *previous*), Embla's `direction: 'rtl'` option (the `dir` attribute alone is not enough), and Radix's `DirectionProvider` — you have 28 Radix packages and zero DirectionProvider, which means the shop's price-range slider will read backwards.
- **Formatting.** Tunisia uses **Western digits** (`129,900`), not Eastern — `Intl` with `ar-TN` already gets this right, but pin `ar-TN-u-nu-latn` as insurance. TND has **three** decimal places; the code hardcodes `toFixed(2)` and a literal `" DT"` in at least 8 places with no central helper. Month names must come from `ar-TN` (`أوت`), not bare `ar` (`أغسطس`) — the Maghreb calendar follows French, and using the wrong one reads as foreign.

**Scale, honestly:** 37 human-facing pages move under `[lang]` (of 40 `page.tsx`), 4 of 5 layouts move, 8 files defining `generateStaticParams` each need a locale dimension, ~830 distinct hardcoded French UI strings in `src/app` need extracting into dictionaries (1,516 site-wide; the excess is SEO marketing prose in `categorySeoTemplates.ts` (241), `tunisiaCategoryKeywords.ts` (132) and `blogSeoConfig.ts` (100), which must be **rewritten** per language, not translated).

### Stage 4 — The Arabic catalogue · **not recommended; costed here so the number is on the table**

Backend: a polymorphic `{model}_translations` table keyed on (type, id, locale), **not** per-locale columns (~126 translatable text columns across 14 tables would become ~252 new columns, and every hand-maintained `SELECT` column list in `ApisController.php:64-106` would triple) and **not** duplicate rows (303 products × 3 sharing one `sku`/`prix`/`qte` would fork the stock truth that `StockMovement` and `InvoiceCalculator` depend on — you fixed that class of bug in PRs #100–101). Per-field fallback to French, so a partial catalogue degrades to French rather than blank. Filament needs per-locale tabs on the 5 resources that matter; today it has zero translation UI, and its only language control is a free-text ISO box on articles that is NULL on all 32 Arabic rows.

Content: **~390,000 words**, human-written or human-rewritten. Category and sub-category pages are not cheap despite there being only 47 — they carry ~26 populated SEO fields each, at ~100% fill rate, which is ~1,200 individual text values on the listing tier alone.

This is a multi-quarter programme. Only Stage 2's data can justify it.

## 5. What NOT to do first, and why

1. **Do not move French to `/fr/`.** It migrates all 391 ranking URLs to buy visual symmetry. There is no traffic upside and the downside is the entire business. French stays at the root and serves as `x-default`.

2. **Do not translate the interface before the content.** Translating the ~830 UI strings while product bodies stay French produces *exactly* the pattern Google names: *"Translating only the boilerplate text of your pages while keeping the bulk of your content in a single language… can create a bad user experience."* And Google's duplicate rule cuts the same way — pages are duplicates *"if the main content of the page remains untranslated."* Chrome-only translation is the worst possible ratio of effort to outcome: all of the engineering cost, none of the SEO value, plus a duplicate-content signal.

3. **Do not flip `MULTILOCALE_ENABLED` to `true`.** The existing switcher is a client-side DOM-walk (`I18nProvider.tsx`, 244 lines, a MutationObserver rewriting text nodes) with dictionaries covering ~48 keys against ~830 strings — under 6%. It creates **zero** indexable URLs, so it is worth exactly nothing for SEO, and it produces visible French/Arabic mixing on screen. The comment in `i18n/index.ts` explaining why it was disabled is correct. Leave it off; Stage 3 replaces it.

4. **Do not start with the RTL codemod.** 386 class occurrences is genuine, satisfying, measurable work — and it is worth zero until an Arabic URL exists to render. It is Stage 3, not Stage 1.

5. **Do not add hreflang yet.** It is a swap mechanism, not a ranking lever, and it does literally nothing on a single-language site — Google ignores non-reciprocal annotations by design. The three degenerate self-referencing blocks you already emit are inert. Fix them when Arabic ships, not before.

6. **Do not machine-translate the catalogue as a "quick test."** Not because of a policy line that no longer exists, but because 303 pages of MT'd manufacturer boilerplate is *unoriginal content at scale* — the thing the current policy actually describes — and because you cannot unpublish a bad first impression from Google's index cheaply. If Arabic is worth doing it is worth writing.

## 6. The first step

**Today, one afternoon: declare the Arabic you already have.**

Set `content_lang = 'ar'` and `content_text_direction = 'rtl'` on the 32 Arabic articles. Give the 31 with broken slugs clean ASCII ones and 301 the old URLs.

Then — and this is the part that matters more than the change itself — **verify it on the live artifact.** `curl https://protein.tn/blog/<the-article-ranking-at-5.73>` and confirm the *served HTML* contains `lang="ar" dir="rtl"`. Not the browser DevTools DOM, which will look right either way because an inline script patches it after paint. The served HTML, before any JavaScript runs. That distinction is the entire difference between this project working and this project shipping green and doing nothing.

If `curl` shows `lang="fr"` on an Arabic article, you have found the real state of Arabic on protein.tn, and you have found it for the cost of one afternoon instead of one quarter.
